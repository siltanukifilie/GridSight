GRIDSIGHT — PRELIMINARY HELD-OUT-SITE EVALUATION OF MEASURED POWER
1 November 2026

SCOPE
This is a retrospective, three-site baseline transfer experiment using KIT data.
It is NOT validation of a pre-construction 20-feature model, Bugesera monthly
demand, annual growth, capacity sizing, or calibrated uncertainty intervals.
Many measurement rows do not replace independent sites: the site count is THREE.
No synthetic observations were generated. Predictions are labelled separately.

DESIGN FIXED BEFORE FITTING
Fold 1: train Congo + Haiti; test Rwanda.
Fold 2: train Rwanda + Haiti; test Congo.
Fold 3: train Rwanda + Congo; test Haiti.
The primary model is an equal-site local-hour profile. For each local hour, take
the mean of qualified measurements separately for each training site, then the
mean of those two site means. Predict that value at the held-out site's local hour.
The comparison model predicts the mean of the two training-site overall means.
Each training site contributes equal weight regardless of its number of hours.
If either training site lacks an hour-of-day value, use the constant baseline.
There is no tuning or model selection based on held-out performance. Both models
are reported even if the more detailed one is worse. No test-site history, mean,
peak, appliance counts, voltage or frequency enters either model.
The original source summaries were already inspected. This is not a blinded or
externally preregistered study. Protocol settings and input hashes are preserved.

TARGET AND UNITS
Target = arithmetic mean of available active-power samples in an eligible UTC
hour, converted from W to kW by division by 1000. It is not a time integral.
Do not sum these power means and claim verified monthly kWh. Duplicate and gap
handling is explicit, but neither exact energy accounting nor billing validation
has been established. Local time is checked against publisher timestamps.

QUALITY RULES
Remove every member of a duplicate timestamp group (48 Congo rows).
Retain finite power samples. Do not fill missing samples/hours with zero.
Use source median cadences fixed before evaluation: 60s Rwanda, 5s Congo, 60s Haiti.
An eligible hour must have 90–110% of expected samples, no internal sample gap
greater than two cadences, and first/last sample within two cadences of its hour
boundaries. Rules apply identically to training and held-out sites.
All calendar hours from first to last timestamp are logged, including completely
missing hours. Exclusion reasons and fractions are reported. Scores apply only
to retained hours, not the excluded periods. Missingness may be non-random.
No filtering by target magnitude or favorable prediction error is used.

METRICS
For each accepted hour, error = predicted kW minus observed kW.
MAE = mean absolute error, kW. Lower is better.
RMSE = square root of mean squared error, kW. Sensitive to large errors.
Bias = mean error, kW. Positive means overprediction; negative means underprediction.
WAPE = 100 x sum(abs(error)) / sum(abs(actual)), percent. It may exceed 100% and
is not an accuracy percentage. Near-zero site demand can produce very large WAPE.
The macro file averages each metric across sites with equal site weight; it is
not a pooled-hour result and the mean RMSE is not a pooled RMSE. Prefer per-site
results when assessing these highly different sites. No calibrated intervals,
p-values or statistical significance claims are produced from three sites.
Metrics are independently checked using scikit-learn, then reconciled against
Excel formulas. A target-poisoning test verifies held-out outcomes cannot change
the fitted baseline parameters. Original dataset files are not modified.

LIMITATIONS THAT MATTER
The recording campaigns cover about 18 days Rwanda, 36 days Congo and 31 days Haiti.
The campaigns are not simultaneous. Training data can postdate the test campaign,
so this is not a chronological deployment backtest.
Rwanda and Congo have publisher-documented damaged/broken equipment and frequency
or voltage constraints. Delivered power is not necessarily unconstrained demand.
Haiti's inverter voltage setting changed during its campaign. Systems and served
loads differ. Transfer error mixes model weakness and differences among systems.
Matched household/business counts, tariffs and pre-connection features are absent.
No Bugesera sector covariates are joined to foreign or unrelated metered labels.
Kigeme is not added to these folds: it is a different institutional case requiring
its own measurement/provenance and comparability assessment.
These findings cannot support a claim that GridSight predicts unbuilt sites well.
The next substantive data need is multiple comparable mini-grids with matched
pre-connection site characteristics and long, quality-controlled consumption data.

REPRODUCE
Install Python libraries in requirements.txt. Run python evaluate.py to reproduce
predictions, metrics and plots from the included compact hourly input.
To rebuild hourly input from the original KIT pack:
python evaluate.py --source-dir ../GridSight_KIT_Metered_Reference_20261101
The original 151 MB KIT ZIP is not duplicated here. This compact pack contains
all hourly inputs needed to reproduce the evaluation results, not raw telemetry.
Recreate Excel with Node and @oai/artifact-tool: node build_workbook.mjs
Excel error/metric formulas recalculate from its fixed prediction table. Editing
Excel cells does not retrain the baselines; use the Python workflow for a new run.
Run python package.py to regenerate the ZIP and per-file integrity inventory.
Run python verify.py to check reproduction, fold separation and scores. Optional:
python verify.py --source-dir ../GridSight_KIT_Metered_Reference_20261101
also verifies that original normalized telemetry files remain unchanged.
The copied site_catalog.csv is source-pack metadata: its hourly_eligible_rows
column counts source samples, NOT scored hours. Use results/data_quality_summary.csv
for this evaluation's stricter hourly coverage and eligibility counts.
