KENYA/NIGERIA UNCERTAINTY AND THREE-YEAR PLANNING

Open Feasibility in the developer or investor dashboard. The view now includes:
* Measured-site error ranges, source scope and downloadable prediction audit.
* The existing 36-month low/base/high curve and annual totals.
* Household affordability inputs and three flat-tariff comparisons.
* One additional mill, pump or cold room at no, half or full assumed use,
  with 36 monthly and three annual results for each alternative.
The printable report and analysis ZIP contain the same applied calculations.

REPRODUCE THE MEASURED STUDY
From the app directory, using the analysis environment:
  .venv/bin/python scripts/build_transfer_uncertainty.py --source "/path/to/data/model_ready/operational_forecasting"
Requires DuckDB, pandas, numpy and scikit-learn. The web app only reads the
small saved JSON/CSV evidence and does not require DuckDB at runtime.

Qualification is checked against the monthly panel at both origin and target.
Observed meter count is not verified active connections. Qualified totals can
include reconstructed intervals. Predictions use the fixed normalized Ridge
from Step 4, with negative predictions floored at zero. The 237 forecast pairs
come from four sites, not 237 independent sites. No synthetic demand is used.

For each outer site/horizon, the other sites are split again: each inner
calibration site is predicted by a model fitted only on remaining sites.
The outer site never enters calibration or fitting. The weighted empirical
90th percentile of absolute errors per origin meter gives the radius; each
calibration site has equal total weight. Final outer predictions are fitted
on all other sites. Lower bounds are clipped at zero.

Reported nested coverage evaluates those test-excluded radii. The pooled
reference radii in summary.json instead describe all outer errors and have
no further independent test. They must not be claimed to achieve the nested
coverage themselves. Months are correlated and there are only 3-4 sites.
This is retrospective site transfer, not a temporal deployment backtest.
Unseen country effects and all previously inspected data limit inference.

The latest-month persistence baseline has lower average site MAE at every
horizon. The study does not select or deploy a winning model. Its ranges
require observed operational history; they are not transferred onto the
pre-construction Bugesera scenario. No 24/36-month calibrated range exists.

GROWTH
Uses the app's active monthly engine, including connection/appliance ramp,
annual growth assumptions, and any entered productive additions. Detailed
mode retains its engineering/seasonality assumptions. Annual energy is the
sum of 12 months; monthly average is annual energy / 12. Low/high are entered
scenario factors, not measured probability bounds. No extra seasonal or
Rwanda demand-growth coefficient is invented from the Kenya/Nigeria study.

PRODUCTIVE USE
Each alternative adds one EXTRA unit to the current plan at the selected
adoption year. Added monthly energy follows the entered business growth;
no uptake/half/full are stress cases, not estimated probabilities. Energy
served is limited by entered monthly supply. Fixed support costs continue
even at zero uptake. Support capital is charged once at launch; unknown
capital keeps launch-year cash unknown. These are incremental developer
cash flows, not enterprise profits. Hourly dispatch, power upgrades and
generation investment remain outside this comparison.

AFFORDABILITY
Enter one household's monthly kWh basket, income and/or energy budget,
fixed fee, and income-share screen in the project currency. Bills are
kWh * tariff + fee at 0.8/1/1.2 times the entered flat tariff. Collection
rate does not reduce household liability. Income share is unavailable at
zero income; the threshold check can still flag a positive bill. Unknown
income, budget or basket remains unknown. The default 5% income screen is
an editable assumption, not a regulatory or survey finding. No price
elasticity, change in uptake, or representative household income is inferred.
