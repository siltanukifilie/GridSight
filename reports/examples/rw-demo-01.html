Plain-language lender explanation

Households account for about 55% of the transparent Year 1 baseline. More customers or more equipment can increase demand, but these counts still need verification.

This view uses the earlier annual-growth reference, not a post-connection adoption curve. Enable connection growth in Feasibility to model early uptake.

Household expansion is assumed at 8% per year and non-household expansion at 12%. These are editable assumptions, not measured trends. Lower use or slower adoption can reduce cash available for debt; faster use can exceed supply.

Public building/service records suggest inputs, not consumption. The model was trained on synthetic data. Check actual customers, utility coverage, productive-user commitments and financing terms before relying on the result. These baseline explanations are not proven causes or exact ML feature attribution.

Safe meter-data sharing proposal

Proposal only — no operator integration or hosted secure portal exists.

  1. Agree the purpose and authority. Operator and project owner approve the evaluation purpose, permitted fields, recipients, retention period and deletion process before sharing. Confirm applicable requirements with the responsible data-protection/legal owner; this proposal is not compliance certification.
  2. Aggregate at the operator. Keep raw customer readings with the operator. Export site-level totals only. Exclude names, phone numbers, meter/account IDs, household coordinates, payment records and interval-level household traces. Review small sites and rare business combinations for disclosure risk; merge or withhold unsafe groups.
  3. Use protected site identifiers. Assign random study site IDs. Keep the mapping to real locations with the operator, separate from the evaluation extract. Replacing names with codes does not guarantee anonymity. Limit country, timing and customer-group details to what the approved study needs.
  4. Freeze independent holdouts. Before training or tuning, split by entire site, ideally also operator. Never put months from one site into both training and test sets. Record the split manifest, model/data versions, coverage, outages, connection dates and input timing. Do not tune on the final held-out results.
  5. Transfer only approved aggregates. Proposed hosted workflow: authenticated, encrypted transfer to a restricted research workspace, named access, audit logs and a fixed deletion deadline. Those controls are not implemented here. This MVP processes the approved CSV locally in memory, does not persist the upload and does not send it to public providers. Do not expose this local server publicly or upload identifiable data.
  6. Publish metrics, not customer data. Return MAE, weighted error, bias and range coverage with site counts and limitations. Review aggregate results before release. Delete working extracts on the agreed schedule and document completion. Current CSV comparison is an annual-average frozen-model diagnostic; validating the new ramp requires separate monthly post-connection records and a held-out study.

Current supported template: anonymous site ID, country, 12 complete historical months' start/end, total customer-side kWh, and the nine matching community inputs. The operator must verify definition/period consistency and avoid identifiable small groups. Do not add extra columns; the importer rejects them.

For a future authorized curve study, prepare restricted site-month aggregates for the first 36 service months, connection month, active customers by group, outages and consistent baseline inputs. These monthly files are not accepted by the current annual CSV uploader.

Proposal references: NIST SP 800-188: de-identification and disclosure-risk governance; NIST Privacy Framework.
